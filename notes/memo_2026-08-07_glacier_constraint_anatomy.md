## 2. The one-reservoir arithmetic — why one excess path cannot serve four masters

For the exponential S_eq, the Nauels law collapses to

  rate = κ · (a − S) · (1 − e^{−b·exc}) · exc^ν,  exc = T_glac − T_eq(S),

and — this is the structural point — **gap and excess are both functions of the single state
S**. Once (a, b, T_off) are pinned by C4+C6+C7 (the SC solve: b 0.282, T_off −0.913,
committed@1850 0.087 m), the excess path is essentially the driver path shifted: 0.67 K
(1900–19) → 1.81 K (2000–23), monotone. The law can only scale this monotone path; the four
demands on it are:

1. C3 wants **high early flow** (0.9 mm/yr at excess 0.7–1.1 K),
2. C2 wants **moderate modern flow** (0.73–0.81 mm/yr at excess 1.8 K),
3. melt-to-date (C4 + the target's cumulative 10.7 cm by 2020) wants the **integral** big,
4. C8 wants **strong excess-sensitivity** (ν ≳ 0.5) so scenarios diverge after 2050.

Demands 1+2 together say the rate must be roughly FLAT while the excess doubles — i.e. weak
excess-sensitivity; demand 4 says strong. Quantitatively (era table, ν=1):

| era | obs rate (mm/yr) | law fit-history (A, κ=0.0053) | law fit-modern-rate (B, κ=0.0020) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1900–19 | 0.89 | 0.24 | 0.12 |
| 1920–49 | 0.96 | 0.61 | 0.30 |
| 1950–79 | 0.52 | 0.39 | 0.24 |
| 1980–99 | 0.54 | 0.57 | 0.35 |
| 2000–23 | 0.73 | **1.17** | 0.69 |

- The law's acceleration demand (2000–23 ÷ 1920–49) is **1.92×**; the data show **0.76×** (a
  deceleration). No κ fixes a ratio.
- Branch A (ML under the standing σ×2 likelihood) splits the difference: it **overshoots the
  best-measured modern segment 1.6–1.7×** (1.36 vs 0.81 mm/yr on 2015–23) AND undershoots the
  early flow ~3× AND still under-melts the century (S(2020) = 86 vs 107 mm — the AR(1) noise
  absorbs the level error).
- Branch B (match the modern rate exactly) destroys everything else: S(2020) = 47 mm (misses
  total melt 2.3×), S(1900) = 7.5 mm (−1.4σ vs Leclercq).
- Raising ν (for C8) multiplies another rising factor onto the path and makes 1-vs-2 worse;
  ν→0 (the pathological/likelihood mode) fixes 1–3 by abandoning 4 and over-committing C6.

**The corner scan kills the "soft anchor" escape:** at the ladder band's floor
(com@1.2K = 11.8%) the SC system has NO solution — C7 (the flow data's own slope) forces more
committed melt than the floor allows. At every solvable anchor (20/28/37.4%) the fitted modern
rate stays 1.36–1.51 mm/yr and S(2020) undershoots (64–86 mm). **The tension is not the
GlacierMIP3 anchor's level; it is the shape mismatch between C1's monotone excess path and
C2/C3's non-monotone rate path, with C8 pulling the sensitivity dial the other way.**

**Why the T1 splits could not fix it:** both pools shared the one driver, the one (b, T_off)
frame, and no per-pool data. The P-split's fast pool must deliver C8's spread (needs large φ·ν
response) while the whole system must keep the modern rate down (needs small response) — the
same dial. The two-Nauels variant (P2) had the right idea (committed ice quiet at modern excess,
mobilized at high excess) but with a shared frame and no per-pool constraints the optimizer
collapsed it to κ_s ≈ κ_f — nothing in the data could tell the pools apart. Feasibility was
0/110 configs under both anchors.

## 3. Where the constraints physically live (the data we already hold)

Per-region anatomy for our scope (GlacierMIP3 S1a/S3 + regchar + Hugonnet 2000–19;
`outputs/diag_constraint_anatomy_regions.csv`):

- **Stock and committed ice live SLOW:** stock-weighted 50%-response time at ~1.5 °C = **513 yr**
  (r19: 828 yr / 33% of stock; r03: 644 yr / 20%); committed-weighted 487 yr.
- **Modern flow lives FAST:** melt-weighted response time = **285 yr**; Alaska (τ = 104 yr) alone
  carries 29% of the 2000–19 melt with 13% of the stock.
- **Response times COLLAPSE with warming:** stock-weighted 513 yr (~1.5 °C) → **125 yr (~3 °C)**
  (r19: 828→213; r03: 644→154). This is GlacierMIP3's own scenario-spread mechanism: strong
  warming mobilizes the slow stock within the century. A fixed-κ single reservoir cannot express
  it; exc^ν imitates it only by also inflating the modern rate (the T1 result).
- **Driver shapes are regionally heterogeneous:** 2000–19 regional warming spans 0.14 K (r16) to
  2.82 K (Svalbard); the ETCW is an Arctic-region feature that area-averaging into one T_glac
  composite mutes (era means 0.51 vs 0.45 K). The early-century flow (C3's high segment, to the
  extent it is real) is an Arctic-regions story that the global driver cannot reproduce without
  the deep-offset pathology. (Caveat: regchar warming is ISIMIP3a GSWP3-W5E5 region-area-weighted,
  not HadCRUT5; treat the r19 value (1.65 K) as uncertain.)

**The aggregation is the structural loss.** The joint constraint set is incompatible for ANY
one-excess-path law, but it decomposes naturally by region: fast, ETCW-forced regions carry the
historical flow shape; slow, high-committed regions carry the stock, the ladder gap, and (via
response-time collapse) the scenario spread — quietly in the hindcast. And unlike the T1 pools,
regional blocks are IDENTIFIED by data we already have on disk: per-region flow (GlaMBIE),
per-region committed anchors (S1a), per-region response times (S1a/regchar/resp_time CSV),
per-region drivers (`t_glac_regions_hadcrut5.csv`, already built).

## 4. Scoping directions (flagged for decision — not resolved)

**T5a — regional blocks (my lead candidate).** 2–4 macro-blocks (e.g. fast: r01+r02+r11-18…;
slow: r19+r03+r09+r04+r07; possibly Arctic-fast vs low-lat-fast split), each with: its own
driver (mass-weighted regional T_glac, already computed per region), its own (a_r, b_r, T_off_r)
disciplined by per-region committed anchors + inventory shares, and its own κ_r with
GlacierMIP3 response-time priors (513→125 collapse informs a mild κ_r(T) or the ν form per
block). Back-of-envelope: slow-block committed ≈ 0.8 × gap ≈ 80 mm; at τ~600 yr it leaks
~0.13 mm/yr today (invisible under C2) but mobilizes ~25–30% under a 585-driven collapse to
τ~150 by late-century → +2–3 cm of 585-vs-126 spread on top of the fast block's ~3–4 → gate
range. The early flow comes from the fast Arctic block's own ETCW excess, no deep offset needed.
This is the physically-real version of P2 WITH the per-pool data that P2 lacked. Cost: an
offline D0-style feasibility cell first (a day), then real calibrator surgery (the biggest item
on the menu — new params ~3–4 per block, new likelihood terms per block).

**T5b — warming-level-dependent transient in one reservoir** (κ(T) or τ(T) fit to the
response-time ladder). Cheap, and honest about the GlacierMIP3 provenance — but it makes the
early-vs-modern shape conflict WORSE on its own (response speeds up with warming ⇒ more
acceleration), so it only works combined with T5d. Worth carrying as the minimal-surgery arm.

**T5c — hybrid hindcast/projection emulator.** Calibrate the transient on history (ν≈0 mode),
switch/blend to a GlacierMIP3 response-time emulator for projections. Preserves the spread
deliverable by construction; costs a regime splice (defensible as "projection physics from the
projection ensemble", but it IS an emulator-of-an-emulator and the splice point is arbitrary).
The fallback if T5a's offline test fails.

**T5d — an explicit early-segment discrepancy model.** Replace the blunt pre-1940 σ×2 with a
structured bias term on the Marzeion-2015-derived segment (e.g. a fitted 1900–1960 offset with
a prior from Roe-2021's critique). This is a DATA-side move on the lowest-confidence constraint
and changes the demanded shape (the lull becomes the anchor, not the early high). Complements
T5a/T5b; on its own it does not create spread.

Not on the menu: T3 (modern-ε inflation — still rejected; it attacks the highest-confidence
constraint), T4 (rejected by Marcus 2026-08-07).
