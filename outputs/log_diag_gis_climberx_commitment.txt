=== 3b. IS CLIMBER-X ADMISSIBLE AS AN EARLY-HORIZON TARGET? (PRIORITY 1 CHECK) ===

  Priority 1 -- historical observations -- outranks priority 4. Before the section-3
  early ratios mean anything, CLIMBER-X has to clear the observed record itself.

  observed GIS loss rate 1995-2024 (recalib_targets_ext.csv): 0.593 mm/yr

    CO2  dT@100yr  CLIMBER-X rate  vs observed
    340      0.41         0.033 mm/yr        0.06x
    380      0.66         0.058 mm/yr        0.10x
    390      0.72         0.064 mm/yr        0.11x
    400      0.77         0.072 mm/yr        0.12x
    420      0.89         0.087 mm/yr        0.15x
    460      1.09         0.117 mm/yr        0.20x

  Its FASTEST first century is 0.117 mm/yr -- 5.1x SLOWER than the observed
  rate, and that is at 1.09 K, close to today's warming.

  ==> CLIMBER-X's 8 km GrIS is NOT admissible as an EARLY-horizon target (threshold: 2x).
      The section-3 'we lose 4x too much in the first millennium' is therefore
      CLIMBER-X being too SLOW, not us being too fast -- its 5.1x deficit against
      observations is the same size as our 4.0x excess against it. It does NOT stack with
      the ISMIP6 2100 finding, and must not be read as confirming it.

=== 4. VERDICT ===

  ONE DEFECT SURVIVES, AND IT IS THE COMMITMENT LAW'S SHAPE.

    * SURVIVES -- the equilibrium. Above ~1.7 K CLIMBER-X commits the whole
      7.5 m sheet; at 2.45 K our linear law commits 0.189 m, 3% of it.
      That is a factor 31, and it is a statement about the SHAPE of L_eq(T): flat below the
      threshold, at the ceiling above it, where ours is a straight line through both.
    * DOES NOT SURVIVE -- the early horizons. Section 3b: this model runs 5.1x below
      the OBSERVED present-day loss rate, so its first millennium cannot be a target.
      Our 4x 'excess' there is its deficit seen from the other side.

  THE EQUILIBRIUM RESULT IS THE (L_eq, tau) DEGENERACY SHOWING. A SMALL reservoir
  emptied FAST fits the observed record as well as a LARGE one emptied SLOWLY, and
  the historical fit -- all c1/c0 ever saw -- cannot separate them. Our calibration
  took the small-fast branch. handoff section 1.1's phi=1 ceiling and section 3.1's
  0/1080 fixed-V reservoirs are the same fact seen from inside the model.

  WHAT IT LICENSES. Not a refit: one model, 9 PARAMETER perturbations of it (not
  structural spread), from the group whose Greenland threshold is a long-standing
  result of that same lineage -- and a model that misses the observed rate by 5.1x
  has not earned a calibration target. Under Marcus's stringency rule this is GUIDANCE.
  What it DOES support is that the linear FORM is wrong, which is structure, not
  calibration, and structure is where a one-model result is allowed to bite.

  NEXT, AND IT IS CHEAP: Greve & Chambers 2022 SICOPOLIS to year 3000 is on disk and
  is an INDEPENDENT model. Two questions for it: does it also put the committed loss
  far above 0.19 m, and does IT clear the observed rate that CLIMBER-X misses?
