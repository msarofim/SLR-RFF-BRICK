====================================================================================================
WHAT THIS ESTABLISHES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
====================================================================================================
  ESTABLISHED  The ssp126 tail EXISTS under the joint band (3.75-6.30% of draws) and
               the 'exactly 0.000 cm in every arm and horizon' statement is a
               FIXED-DRIVER statement that does not survive the band we now report.
  ESTABLISHED  At 2100 and 2150 the tipped fraction is BELOW the p95 cut, so the
               scored statistic is arithmetically blind to the tail. The 0.24-0.33x
               'under-dispersion' is therefore NOT evidence of a missing tail.
  NOT SETTLED  Whether 3.95% is the RIGHT tipped fraction at ssp126@2100. That is a
               physical question about marine ice-sheet instability under low forcing
               and it needs a literature comparison this repo does not have.
  ⇒ THE QUESTION CHANGES from 'why is our band narrow' (a WIDTH problem, and the fix
    would be to widen it) to 'is our tipped FRACTION right' (a THRESHOLD problem,
    with a different fix and a different piece of evidence needed).
  ⚠ AND THE MAGNITUDE-DEPENDENT FORK DOES NOT ADDRESS EITHER OF THEM AT ssp126.
    g = (excess/ref)^n is ZERO below threshold for every n, so it changes nothing
    for the 96% of ssp126 draws that never tip. It is the ssp245/ssp585 SEPARATION
    fix (ais_binary_form_priced), not the ssp126 one. Two different binary features:
    binary in MAGNITUDE (the fork fixes it) and binary in ONSET (it does not).
