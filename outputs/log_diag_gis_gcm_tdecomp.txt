  R2 = across-GCM variance in the ISM 2300 level explained by that route; 'rank r' is
  Spearman, which does not assume linearity. 'resid sd / ISM sd' = the fraction of the
  band's own width surviving the best linear rescale of that predictor — the IRREDUCIBLE
  share: precipitation / SMB + ISM structure + member, which this cannot separate further.

  ** n IS SMALL. ** The only arm with enough GCMs to fit is ssp585 r2300, and it has 4 — a 2-parameter fit on
  4 points. Read R2 as indicative, not as an estimate with an interval. The two COOL
  arms have 2 GCMs each and CANNOT be assessed this way at all — which includes ssp245,
  the arm where CESM2 was load-bearing in diag_gis_scorecard_logo.py.

  0.63-1.51 and `north` at 1.39-2.06 — so the law applies a NORTH-sized
  amplification to a SOUTH-zone driver, over-driving it by 1.38x at the median.

  This is a DIRECT measurement of section 4.2's sub-choice 1, on the very models
  PROTECT used: the flat-hold's 1.64 against a measured 1.19. It does NOT
  by itself mean the emulator is wrong — c1 was calibrated against the OBSERVED south
  driver and absorbs the level — which is exactly why the DIRECT routes above UNDER-predict.

=== THE ANSWER TO THE BAND-BASIS QUESTION ===

  On the one arm that can be fitted, OUR OWN production drive route explains R2 = 0.92
  of the across-GCM variance, and only 28% of the band's width survives it. So most
  of what the raw band calls uncertainty is LOCAL TEMPERATURE the emulator already tracks,
  not ice-sheet disagreement.

  ==> Build the band on the RESIDUAL after regressing out our own prediction, not on
      raw ISM output — neither run-level nor GCM-clustered quantiles of the raw runs.
      Run-level and GCM-clustered were the WRONG two options; both quantile the total.
